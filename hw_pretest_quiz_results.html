HOMEWORK TO HAND IN ON PAPER:
Problems are listed as chapter.problem thus 1.2 is Chapter 1, problem 2. Starred problems (announced after homework is collected) are graded for being correct and can get bonus points. *You must show your work for any math.*

*Give traceable references/citations (URLs) for any information you use not from the textbook/lectures, cite using
quotation marks, indicate paraphrasing and give sources for numbers you give (even if from the textbook/lectures).*

 You are expected to write at college level. If there are too many errors in basic grammar, spelling, capitalization or punctuation, points
can be taken off. If you get stuck and don't know how to approach a problem, say where you got stuck. 
That's perfectly OK for doing some effort.  If you have no idea where to begin, say so, so that we can work to get you
on track.

The point of homework is to  train for tests, so even if you hand in homework and say you don't know,
you're expected to ask for help at some point, even after the fact.  With all this said, honesty will be rewarded.
It's better to be honest and say you don't know than to copy/plagiarize and get written up for it.

Star-Rise Problem:  Explain why stars rise 4 minutes earlier every day, and MAKE A SKETCH
to illustrate your point.
Constellation Problem:  In January from Louisville, the Shawnee constellation of the Caribou is
 on the eastern horizon at 6pm LOCAL time.
(Note that local time is about 40 minutes earlier than Eastern Standard Time, because Louisville
is on the far western end of the eastern time zone and in fact should not even be on eastern time.
In Louisville, the sun is at its highest point in the sky at noon LOCAL time, or 12:40pm EST.)
Where (in detail) would the Caribou be at midnight LOCAL time?  Explain why. 

Poisson (Counting) Problem:
a) In Louisville Metro (pop. about 1 million), you poll 10 people for an election.  Seven people choose the green party, and
three people choose the yellow party.
i) What is the percentage for each party?
ii) What is the one sigma (standard deviation) error for the numbers for the green and yellow parties.  Convert this into the
range of percentage preferred for each party.  Can you make a conclusion about the election?  Explain your answer.

b) Now you poll 2000 people for an election.  1400 people choose the green party, and 600 people choose
the yellow party.
i) What is the percentage for each party?
ii) What is the one sigma (standard deviation) error for the numbers for the green and yellow parties.  Convert this into the
range of percentage preferred for each party.   Can you make a conclusion about the election?  Explain your answer.  If your answer
is different from (a), explain why.

VLA Problem:
