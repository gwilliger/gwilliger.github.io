Constellation Problem:  In January from Louisville, the Shawnee constellation the Caribou is
on the eastern horizon at 6pm LOCAL time.
(Note that local time is about 40 minutes earlier than Eastern Standard Time, because Louisville
is on the far western end of the eastern time zone and in fact should not even be on eastern time.
In Louisville, the sun is at its highest point in the sky at noon LOCAL time, or 12:40pm EST.)
Where (in detail) would the Caribou be at midnight LOCAL time?  Explain why. 

Poisson (Counting) Problem:
a) In Louisville Metro (pop. about 1 million), you poll 10 people for an election.  Seven people choose the green party, and
three people choose the yellow party.
i) What is the percentage for each party?
ii) What is the one sigma (standard deviation) error for the numbers for the green and yellow parties.  Convert this into the
range of percentage preferred for each party.  Can you make a conclusion about the election?  Explain your answer.

b) Now you poll 2000 people for an election.  1400 people choose the green party, and 600 people choose
the yellow party.
i) What is the percentage for each party?
ii) What is the one sigma (standard deviation) error for the numbers for the green and yellow parties.  Convert this into the
range of percentage preferred for each party.   Can you make a conclusion about the election?  Explain your answer.  If your answer
is different from (a), explain why.

Pandemic Problem:
a) The number of people a COVID-19 positive person infects is the R-value. Assume a person is infectious for ONE WEEK.  If R=1, a person infects one other
person, if R=2, a person infects 2 other people, etc.    Start with one
infectious person.
i) If R=1, how many total people were infected after 1 week, after 2 weeks, after 3 weeks and after 4 weeks?
ii) If R=4, how many total people were infected after 1 week, after 2 weeks after 3 weeks and after 4 weeks?
iii) How much does the total number of people infected change vs. the R-value?

NOTE: See this article for background:
Coronavirus: What is the R number and how is it calculated?
James Gallagher, BBC, 22 Jan 2021

b) Assume a COVID-19 positivity rate of 19% (the positivity rate in Jefferson County as of 24 Aug 2022, see
https://govstatus.egov.com/ky-covid-data-dashboard).  You invite people from a random list to a party.
How big a group do you need before there is at least a 50% chance of having at least one positive person at your party?
