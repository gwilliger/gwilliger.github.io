Homework policy for Physics & Astronomy 107 - online version, revised 2020.12.01

Each homework assignment is roughly 1-1.5% of your grade (recoupable with extra credit), so missing one or two should not hurt
you too badly. The *real* payoff to doing homework is learning how to do the
problems, because most of your grade will be based on performance on quizzes
and tests.  So, it's in your interest to learn how to do the problems, and to
look over the answers once they're posted.  If you don't understand them, get
help from a classmate, or one among a University tutor, A-star or Dr. W.

Homework grades depend on attempting a question, not whether answers are right or not:
Your score will be the  number of questions attempted, and up to two additional
points can be given if a randomly chosen problem is done right. 
(Which question that is will be announced after the homework is collected.)
The grader has additional discretion to give extra points for answering the problems
very well.
The lowest homework score will be dropped.  You need no excuse.  If you are ill or cannot do it, that is enough reason and you need not say why.

As a policy, I do not answer homework questions on the day they are due I am happy to talk about homework up through the day before it is due. I prefer
1) talking face to face, and if that is not possible, then
2) making an appointment to talk by telephone or videocall.
I strongly discourage corresponding with students about homework via e-mail, because it becomes very difficult to draw the answer out of students through dialogue.


Be neat and keep one equals sign per line, and keep track of units.
(It helps you to find when you make a mistake!)
You are encouraged to work together, but you must turn in your own work. Copying is not permitted!
If you work with one or more partners, please give their names.

Policy for Copying Homework, Plagiarism and Other Forms of Cheating
1) If the instructor learns that anyone brings forward evidence that homework is copied (answers much more
identical between two or more homework/tests etc. than would be expected with
randomly chosen pairs of students in the class) or that a student has plagiarized (copied from any outside source
and passed it off as his/her own work), penalties will be assessed with
discretion and on a case-by-case basis. This can include up to
a zero for that homework AND ALL PRECEDING HOMEWORK,
or A FAILING GRADE FOR THE COURSE.
This is to encourage a cessation of copying as early in the course as possible.
A student who turns himself/herself in will receive more lenient penalties.

2) If over 20% of the assigned problems for homework (or over 20% of any other assignment)
shows evidence of plagiarism, copying, cheating etc., penalties WILL  be greater than
a zero for the affected work/problems.  Penalties can include a zero for ALL of the homework in the course,
ALL of a term paper, an extra reduction in course grade or even FAILURE IN THE COURSE.
POLICY FOR CHEATING WITH CLICKERS (when used)
If a student intentionally uses another student's clicker with that student's knowledge, in particular if the other student is absent, this is a clear case of
academic misconduct. 
FIRST OFFENSE: Both owners of the clickers will receive zeroes for the day, and cumulative zeroes for participation up through that day.
SECOND OFFENSE: Zero for participation for the entire semester, and a letter to the Dean of Arts & Sciences.
THIRD OFFENSE: Failure in the course.
If a student intentionally uses another student's clicker without that student's knowledge, this use will be immediately reported to the Dean and University Police,
and in addition the same penalties as above will apply about loss of participation points for the student who used the clicker.
A student who turns himself/herself in will receive more lenient penalties.

 
POLICY FOR CHEATING ON QUIZZES AND TESTS

Sanctions will be done on a case-by-case basis.  A minimum penalty will be a zero on the test
(mini-quiz, quiz, big quiz - or for those courses or individuals with a final, on the final).
My maximum penalty is failure of the course.
I use cheating detection software and will report cases of cheating to the Dean's office.
The University has the power to suspend or expel students in case of academic misconduct.
See the Student Handbook for details.

A student who turns himself/herself in will receive more lenient penalties.
