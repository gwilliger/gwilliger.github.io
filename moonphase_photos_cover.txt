Photos should be taken within 3 days of new, first quarter, full and third
quarter.  For new Moon, you need to be at least one day off to get some
crescent. Be sure to follow the directions on the class website to take useful
photos.  You may not be able to  take the photos in order because of weather
problems, so photos taken out of order are fine.  Altitude should be to within 5
degrees.  Your fist at arm's length should subtend about 10-20 degrees.  (Check
it with your planisphere -- it has a protractor!  
If it's not, then estimate the altitude using the protractor on your
planisphere.)  Azimuth
should be one of N,NE,E,SE,S,SW,W,NW.  Use a compass, map, GPS or any other
system you like to determine azimuth.  Use the (general) extra credit site link for 
right ascension (RA) and declination (dec). Use the same camera and lens for all
observations.  Observing conditions means clear, cloudy, hazy, partly cloudy etc.

The Moon's daily right ascension (RA) and declination (dec) are listed 
in the ephemeris at

Use the website http://astropixels.com/ephemeris/ephemeris.html
and click on "Geocentric Ephemerides for the Sun, Moon and planets: 2021 to 2030"
then on the current year.

CAUTION:  The date, RA and dec on that website are for 03 hours UT (Universal 
Time or Greenwich Mean Time) (10pm EST or 11pm EDT the night before, depending
on whether we are on daylight saving time or not). This means that the DATE on
the chart is ONE DAY LATER than for Louisville if you are observing before
midnight!  You can interpolate the RA and dec if you like for your time of
observation, but give your calculation if you do so.
