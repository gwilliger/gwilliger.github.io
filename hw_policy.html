Homework policy for Physics & Astronomy 107

Each homework assignment is roughly 1% of your grade (recoupable with extra credit), so missing one or two should not hurt
you too badly. The *real* payoff to doing homework is learning how to do the
problems, because most of your grade will be based on performance on quizzes
and tests.  So, it's in your interest to learn how to do the problems, and to
look over the answers once they're posted.  If you don't understand them, get
help from a classmate, or one among a University tutor, A-star or Dr. W.

Homework grades depend on attempting a question, not whether answers are right or not:
Your score will be the  number of questions attempted, and up to two additional
points can be given if a randomly chosen problem is done right. 
(Which question that is will be announced after the homework is collected.)
The grader has additional discretion to give extra points for answering the problems
very well.
The lowest homework score will be dropped.

As a policy, I do not answer homework questions on the day they are due I am happy to talk about homework up through the day before it is due. I prefer
1) talking face to face, and if that is not possible, then
2) making an appointment to talk by telephone.
I strongly discourage corresponding with students about homework via e-mail, because it becomes very difficult to draw the answer out of students through dialogue.


Be neat and keep one equals sign per line, and keep track of units.
(It helps you to find when you make a mistake!)
You are encouraged to work together, but you must turn in your own work. Copying is not permitted!
If you work with one or more partners, please give their names. 


Homework policy - to help us work with up to 150 homework sets per week!:
1) no frizzy paper -- must have clean edge, not ragged from tearing out of a spiral
    notebook (this makes a mess as pieces come off, and makes papers stick together)
2) must be on standard size 8.5x11 inch paper
3) multiple pages must be stapled
4) name, due date, homework set (for example HW4) on upper right
5) use the problem numbering convention 1.7 for Chapter 1, problem 7 (so the grader and I
can easily tell which problem it is)
6) Please do *not* e-mail in your assignment or turn it in early unless you clear it with me --
please hand it in on paper when it's due. The class is simply too big to have assignments coming in at
different times and to have to open e-mails, detach attachments and print them out for grading. I
can work out individual alternatives in emergencies if the need arises. Please make arrangements
with me first.
7) Homework is due on time.  When homework is due, I post answers  to the class
-- in class and soon afterward on the protected website.  Late homework won't be accepted. 
It's not fair to those who got it in on time to accept it late. To soften the blow, the lowest
homework score will be dropped.  You can e-mail it to me with advance notice if you can get it
(see above, pdf preferred, no Word or Excel or Microsoft format files), fax it or send
it ahead with a friend if you can't make it.  I can consider excusing you from homework
past your one dropped homework only if  you have a written, verifiable excuse (illness,
death in family etc., similar to excuses for tests.)


     This is for your protection, to reduce the chance that  papers will stick
together, someone else will take it, there will be a misreading etc. 
     It is your responsibility to pick up your homework (and tests!) and to
check if there is a grading error.  (Despite our best efforts, if there is a 1%
error rate, someone will have a problem in a class of 150.)  If a
homework set is not in the proper format, then you may lose credit, and especially so if
any sheets get lost.  Homework counts for enough of the grade to make a difference of 1/3 to 2/3
of a letter grade.

     Homework sets and quizzes/tests more than one week old may not necessarily
be brought to class, but may be picked up in my office.

Here are more homework policies from our grader in Spring 2008, Jeremy Hornbeck,
which will be followed this semester:

1)  Half hearted answers get half credit (even if they are correct).  It is YOUR
responsibility to show that you know how to do the problem, so SHOW YOUR WORK.

2)  Obvious copying from others will result in loss of credit.  Do not copy verbatim from the
textbook, Wikipedia or any other source, either. That is PLAGIARISM and is academic misconduct,
which will be reported to the Dean of Arts & Sciences. You must *CITE* sources (for text, figures etc.), with proper documentation,
to *justify* your own work.   Answer in your own words.
Details on the copying/cheating policy are below.

3)  Excellence on homework will be rewarded with one or more extra points.

4)  Illegible or messy work will be penalized (no staple, messy edges, can't
read, etc.).

5)  Please TRY.  If you are unsure, try anyway.  I can tell rather quickly
who put effort into the problems and who didn't.  A correct answer without
shown work might as well be a wrong answer to me, and a wrong answer with
good work behind it will be counted right.  I want to know you are trying to
learn the material.  Full credit will be given to people who try hard on
every problem regardless of their answers.

Policy for Copying Homework, Plagiarism and Other Forms of Cheating
1) If anyone brings to my attention that homework is copied (answers much more
identical between two or more homework/tests etc. than would be expected with
randomly chosen pairs of students in the class) or that a student has plagiarized (copied from another source and passed it off as his/her own work), penalties will be assessed with
discretion and on a case-by-case basis.  General guidelines are:
FIRST OFFENSE: COPYING: If students identify their working partner(s), zero. 
If students do NOT identify their working partner,
zero credit and -1% on grade.
PLAGIARISM: same as copying.
SECOND OFFENSE: COPYING: If students identify their working partner(s), zero credit and -1%
on participation grade.
If students do not identify their working partner(s), zero credit and -2% on the participation grade.
PLAGIARISM: same as copying
THIRD OFFENSE: COPYING: If students identify their working partner(s), zero credit, -3%
on the participation grade and a letter to the Dean's office.  If students do not identify
their working partner(s), zero credit, -5% on the participation grade and a letter to the Dean's
office.  In either case, the grading contract is voided and the instructor will determine
the student's course grade as he sees fit.
PLAGIARISM: Failure in the course and a report to the Dean of Arts & Sciences.

A student who turns himself/herself in will receive more lenient penalties.

POLICY FOR CHEATING WITH CLICKERS
If a student intentionally uses another student's clicker with that student's knowledge, in particular if the other student is absent, this is a clear case of
academic misconduct. 
FIRST OFFENSE: Both owners of the clickers will receive zeroes for the day, and cumulative zeroes for participation up through that day.
SECOND OFFENSE: Zero for participation for the entire semester, and a letter to the Dean of Arts & Sciences.
THIRD OFFENSE: Failure in the course.
If a student intentionally uses another student's clicker without that student's knowledge, this use will be immediately reported to the Dean and University Police,
and in addition the same penalties as above will apply about loss of participation points for the student who used the clicker.
A student who turns himself/herself in will receive more lenient penalties.

 
POLICY FOR CHEATING ON QUIZZES AND TESTS

Sanctions will be done on a case-by-case basis.  A minimum penalty will be a zero on the test
(mini-quiz, quiz, big quiz - or for those courses or individuals with a final, on the final).
My maximum penalty is failure of the course.
I use cheating detection software and will report cases of cheating to the Dean's office.
The University has the power to suspend or expel students in case of academic misconduct.
See the Student Handbook for details.

A student who turns himself/herself in will receive more lenient penalties.
